## Review par IMDOOMED · LV 14

*1mth (08/10/2026) · 17 likes · 27 replies*

It is genuinely heartbreaking to watch a JoJo fanfiction that was so remarkably well-written up to this point turn into yet another fraudulent crossover, purely because the author suddenly abandoned the most sacred rule of Hirohiko Araki’s universe! After he entered into jujutsu kaisen universe!

How many times do crossover writers need to be reminded that the nature of a Stand has absolutely zero relation to standard fictional energies or visual gifts? It does not matter how immense a character’s mental power is, what tier their spiritual awareness reaches, how refined their cursed energy might be, or whether they possess extraordinary ocular abilities allowing them to see the invisible and pierce through the deepest secrets of their universe: even if a character ascends to godhood or becomes semi-omniscient, the golden rule of JoJo is absolute: only Stand users can see Stands. It is not a suggestion, it is the very essence of this power! For any non-Stand user, the Stand is fundamentally a broken ability that respects no rules and is an entirely imperceptible force: during battle, an opponent can at best register indirect physical phenomena like sudden air displacement, concrete shattering under invisible blows, or an inexplicable invisible presence, but they will never see a silhouette and will have no clue what is actually striking them.

What makes this slip-up so baffling is that the author handled this exact boundary with absolute perfection in the previous worlds like Hellsing and Baki. So by what bizarre logic, upon arriving in Jujutsu Kaisen, a world with zero intrinsic compatibility for awakening Stands, does the author suddenly decide that every sorcerer can casually lay eyes on the Stand and even downgrade it to a generic cursed spirit? Equating the direct manifestation of a user’s life energy with a curse born of negative human emotion proves that the author does not truly understand the masterpiece they set out to adapt. Everything was fantastic up until this arc, but this is where the story completely starts going off the rails.
